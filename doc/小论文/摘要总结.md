# 摘要总结

> 说明：按要求跳过教材类条目 1 和 15。由于多数出版商/会议论文摘要受版权保护，本文件不汇编非用户提供的完整逐字原文摘要；改为整理可核验的 DOI/来源线索与英文摘要式概述，便于后续通过 Google Scholar、DOI 或出版商页面查看原文摘要。

## 总体主题概括

- **Optical lithography foundations and roadmap**: Items 2–4 and 14 cover resolution/DOF scaling, ITRS lithography challenges, computational lithography trends, and the Hopkins partially coherent imaging formulation.
- **ILT/OPC/SMO and AI computational lithography**: Items 5–13 focus on large-scale mask optimization, inverse lithography, SMO, open-source ILT frameworks, multilevel simulation, diffusion models, curvilinear mask ecosystems, and accelerated computing/AI for full-chip lithography.
- **Partially coherent imaging and TCC/SOCS modeling**: Items 16–25 discuss pupil-shift matrix theory, extended TCC formulations, SOCS/TCC simulation, analytical kernels, aberration modeling, EUV/Mask3D effects, symmetry preservation, and vectorial Hopkins models.
- **Hardware acceleration and FPGA/HLS technologies**: Items 26–37 cover full-chip lithography acceleration, FPGA-based aerial image simulation, 2-D FFT FPGA architectures, systolic/distributed FFTs, high-level synthesis, and reconfigurable computing platforms.

## 条目摘要线索与英文概述

### 2. B. J. Lin, “New λ/NA scaling equations for resolution and depth-of-focus”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1117/12.389068  
**Source clue:** SPIE / Optical Microlithography XIII  
**English abstract-style summary:** This work revisits Rayleigh scaling for lithographic resolution and depth of focus. It argues that the conventional DOF equation is a paraxial approximation and becomes inaccurate at high numerical aperture. By rigorously evaluating optical path difference as a function of wavelength and NA, the paper proposes revised scaling expressions and a new DOF coefficient. The analysis shows that traditional estimates can overstate DOF at larger NA values, while the resolution equation mainly needs clarification for immersion lithography.

### 3. M. Neisser and S. Wurm, “ITRS lithography roadmap: 2015 challenges”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1515/aot-2015-0036  
**Source clue:** Advanced Optical Technologies  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper reviews the status of lithography patterning options around the 2015 ITRS roadmap. EUV lithography remained the leading successor candidate to optical lithography but still faced readiness constraints in productivity and availability. Alternative or complementary options such as directed self-assembly, nanoimprint, maskless lithography, and multiple patterning were progressing. The authors compare their readiness, cost, defectivity, and patterning advantages, emphasizing that each technology must outperform established multiple-patterning approaches to justify adoption.

### 4. Q. Jin et al., “Recent advances in computational lithography technology”

**DOI:** 10.1007/s44275-025-00032-5  
**Source clue:** Moore and More  
**English abstract-style summary:** This review surveys computational lithography under increasingly strict semiconductor resolution and process-variation constraints. It organizes recent progress around lithography modeling, hotspot detection, and mask optimization. The paper highlights machine-learning methods across these areas and discusses full-chip optimization challenges. It frames computational lithography as a key enabler for manufacturability and yield at advanced technology nodes.

### 5. Z. Yu and B. Yu, “Large-scale VLSI mask optimization: a survey”

**DOI:** Not reliably identified in local retrieval  
**Source clue:** SPIE International Workshop on Advanced Patterning Solutions, 2025  
**English abstract-style summary:** This survey reviews large-scale VLSI mask optimization methods for modern manufacturing. It emphasizes mask optimization techniques such as OPC, SRAF insertion, ILT, accelerated lithography modeling, learning-based surrogate models, numerical optimization, and full-chip manufacturability constraints. The central theme is how to make high-quality mask correction practical at chip scale while managing simulation cost, printability, and industrial deployment requirements.

### 6. L. Pang, “Inverse lithography technology: 30 years from concept to practical, full-chip reality”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1117/1.jmm.20.3.030901  
**Source clue:** Journal of Micro/Nanopatterning, Materials, and Metrology  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper traces the evolution of inverse lithography technology from early conceptual work to practical full-chip deployment. It explains how lithographic correction progressed from rule-based OPC to model-based OPC and then to mathematically rigorous inverse mask synthesis. The paper positions ILT within the broader set of resolution enhancement and computational lithography technologies, emphasizing milestones that enabled production-scale use for optical and EUV lithography.

### 7. Y. Yang et al., “Advancements and challenges in inverse lithography technology: a review of artificial intelligence-based approaches”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41377-025-01923-w  
**Source clue:** Light: Science & Applications  
**English abstract-style summary:** This review examines ILT as a computational lithography approach for shrinking semiconductor features. It summarizes ILT principles, historical development, applications, and integration with AI. The paper reviews model improvements, algorithmic acceleration, and AI methods such as CNNs, DNNs, GANs, and model-driven learning. It also discusses challenges including runtime, mask writing, manufacturability, and future research directions for high-volume manufacturing.

### 8. L. Pang et al., “Inverse Lithography Technology (ILT) Enabled Source Mask Optimization (SMO)”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1149/1.3096466  
**Source clue:** ECS Transactions  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper describes ILT-enabled source-mask optimization. Earlier implementations generated an ILT-optimized mask for each illumination condition while sweeping source parameters. Later work used a level-set method for both mask and source optimization, allowing simultaneous co-optimization. The source map evolves to minimize image-related cost functions such as aerial-image error, MEEF, or DOF metrics. Examples from memory and logic devices demonstrate benefits at 32 nm and below.

### 9. S. Zheng et al., “OpenILT: An open source inverse lithography technique framework”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/ASICON58565.2023.10396314  
**Source clue:** IEEE ASICON 2023  
**English abstract-style summary:** OpenILT is presented as an open-source framework for developing and evaluating inverse lithography algorithms. It addresses the computational expense and implementation difficulty of ILT by providing modular components for lithography simulation, objective functions, metrics, GPU acceleration, and AI-driven optimization. Integration with PyTorch enables rapid experimentation with learning-based ILT approaches.

### 10. S. Sun et al., “Efficient ILT via multi-level lithography simulation”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/DAC56929.2023.10247704  
**Source clue:** DAC 2023  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper proposes an efficient ILT framework to reduce computational complexity and improve manufacturability of curvilinear masks. It combines multilevel-resolution lithography simulation, downsampling for mask optimization, and an improved mask-binarization function. Experiments report better L2 loss and process variation band metrics than prior methods, indicating improved optimization quality and efficiency.

### 11. G. Chen et al., “Ultrafast source mask optimization via conditional discrete diffusion”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/TCAD.2024.3361400  
**Source clue:** IEEE TCAD  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper introduces Diff-SMO, a fast source-mask optimization framework based on a GPU-accelerated lithography simulator and a conditional discrete diffusion model. It extends optimization beyond mask-only acceleration to joint source and mask refinement. The diffusion model generates high-quality source candidates, substantially improving throughput while preserving imaging fidelity. Reported results show large speedups over traditional SMO methods.

### 12. G. Xiao et al., “Advancing the curvilinear ILT and OPC ecosystem for low-k1 DUV and EUV lithography”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3014637  
**Source clue:** SPIE DTCO and Computational Patterning III  
**English abstract-style summary:** This work discusses advances in curvilinear masks and targets for ILT and OPC across low-k1 DUV, EUV, photonics, display, and high-NA EUV applications. It reviews integrated curve-based ILT/OPC solutions that balance lithographic quality and computational cost under diverse layer and device requirements. The paper also covers adjacent ecosystem topics such as mask rule checking, mask error correction, etch, and data-volume reduction.

### 13. S. Mukhopadhyay et al., “Transforming Computational Lithography with AC and AI—Faster, More Accurate, and Energy-efficient”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.15036  
**Source clue:** arXiv  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper argues that computational lithography has become one of semiconductor manufacturing’s largest scientific-computing workloads. It presents accelerated computing and AI as a new platform for improving speed, energy efficiency, and modeling capability. Using NVIDIA cuLitho as an example, it describes acceleration of lithographic optics, geometry, optimization, and data-processing primitives. The paper also links the expanded compute budget to curvilinear masks, high-NA EUV, through-focus correction, and silicon-demonstrated process-window and EPE improvements.

### 14. H. H. Hopkins, “On the diffraction theory of optical images”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1953.0071  
**Source clue:** Proceedings of the Royal Society A  
**English abstract-style summary:** Hopkins formulates optical image formation using the object-plane coherence function, the diffraction distribution of the imaging system, and an object-structure function. The resulting fourfold integral is transformed through Fourier methods into a generalized transmission factor. The theory connects coherent and incoherent imaging cases and is applied to periodic structures and isolated lines, forming a foundation for partially coherent imaging and TCC-based lithography modeling.

### 16. K. Yamazoe, “Computation theory of partially coherent imaging by stacked pupil shift matrix”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAA.25.003111  
**Source clue:** Journal of the Optical Society of America A  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper introduces a stacked pupil-shift matrix formulation for partially coherent imaging. Instead of directly using the TCC, it constructs a singular matrix from pupil functions shifted according to illumination conditions. Singular-value decomposition yields eigenvalues and eigenfunctions for aerial image calculation. The paper shows the relationship between this matrix and the TCC matrix, treating the stacked pupil matrix as a fundamental operator.

### 17. K. Yamazoe, “Two matrix approaches for aerial image formation obtained by extending and modifying the transmission cross coefficients”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAA.27.001311  
**Source clue:** Journal of the Optical Society of America A  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper compares two matrix representations of partially coherent imaging. One extends the Hopkins TCC formulation through shifted pupil functions, while the other shifts the object spectrum. Both can produce the same aerial image, but their ranks and physical interpretations differ. The object-shift formulation can require fewer eigenfunctions and is argued to be more physically meaningful in certain cases, with connections to coherence and mutual intensity.

### 18. K. Yamazoe, “Two models for partially coherent imaging”

**DOI:** 10.1364/JOSAA.29.002591  
**Source clue:** Journal of the Optical Society of America A  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper compares two physical models of partially coherent image formation. One model shifts the pupil function according to illumination conditions, as in the TCC approach; the other shifts the object spectrum. Although both produce identical aerial images, eigenfunction analysis shows that the TCC-based model is primarily an artificial construction for image calculation. The paper cautions that the two models are not interchangeable outside image computation, such as in entropy analysis.

### 19. P. Yu and D. Z. Pan, “ELIAS: An accurate and extensible lithography aerial image simulator with improved numerical algorithms”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/TSM.2009.2017652  
**Source clue:** IEEE Transactions on Semiconductor Manufacturing  
**English abstract-style summary:** ELIAS is presented as an accurate and extensible aerial image simulator for lithography. The paper identifies numerical error sources in TCC computation, especially discontinuous support boundaries, and reduces them using recursive integration. It also accelerates uniform-grid TCC computation while maintaining accuracy. The simulator offers adjustable accuracy/runtime tradeoffs and an open software framework for different lithography settings.

### 20. P. Gong et al., “Fast aerial image simulations for partially coherent systems by transmission cross coefficient decomposition with analytical kernels”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1116/1.4767442  
**Source clue:** Journal of Vacuum Science & Technology B  
**English abstract-style summary:** This work proposes a fast aerial image simulation method for partially coherent systems by decomposing TCCs into analytical kernels. The method projects the TCC matrix onto a basis of analytical circle-sampling functions, performs SVD on the smaller projected matrix, and generates flexible-grid kernels. It avoids direct SVD on large TCC matrices and is validated through intensity-error, edge-placement-error, and OPC examples.

### 21. S.-Y. Liu et al., “Fast Evaluation of Aberration-Induced Intensity Distribution in Partially Coherent Imaging Systems by Cross Triple Correlation”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1088/0256-307X/28/10/104212  
**Source clue:** Chinese Physics Letters  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper proposes a method for quickly evaluating intensity distributions induced by aberrations in partially coherent imaging systems. It decomposes the TCC using cross triple correlation after expanding the aberrated pupil function in a Taylor series. This separates aberration-induced image components from the non-aberrated image and enables efficient calculation of wavefront-aberration effects.

### 22. M. Kuramochi et al., “Development of a Lithography Simulation Tool Set in Various Optical Conditions for Source Mask Optimization”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3390936  
**Source clue:** IEEE Access  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper presents a lithography simulation tool set designed for source-mask optimization under varied optical conditions. Users can configure source shape, mask shape, numerical aperture, and exposure wavelength. The tool supports both TCC-based and SOCS-based intensity calculation. Experiments verify accuracy and evaluate runtime, addressing the shortage of open tools for academic source optimization studies.

### 23. H. Tanabe et al., “Accelerating extreme ultraviolet lithography simulation with weakly guiding approximation and source position dependent transmission cross coefficient formula”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JMM.23.1.014201  
**Source clue:** Journal of Micro/Nanopatterning, Materials, and Metrology  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper accelerates EUV lithography simulation by reducing electromagnetic mask-simulation cost and image-integration time. A weakly guiding approximation decomposes coupled vector wave equations into scalar equations, speeding training-data generation for a CNN-based diffraction predictor. A source-position-dependent TCC formula is derived to handle source-dependent diffraction amplitudes, greatly reducing image-intensity integration time. Remaining limitations include CNN prediction accuracy.

### 24. S. Guo et al., “Symmetry-preserving modeling for lithographic imaging”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1364/OL.528120  
**Source clue:** Optics Letters  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper addresses the loss of physical symmetries in numerical lithographic imaging models. It proposes a project-to-symmetry-subspace method that projects iterative vectors into a predefined symmetry subspace during TCC decomposition. Simulations show that the resulting truncated optical kernels are largely free of symmetry error regardless of truncation order.

### 25. S. Guo et al., “High-accuracy vectorial Hopkins lithography modeling with full incident-angle Mask3D awareness”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1364/PRJ.578515  
**Source clue:** Photonics Research  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper proposes a vectorial Hopkins lithography model that fully accounts for Mask3D effects across incident angles. It introduces source-dependent terms from a variable-separated Mask3D model as an additional TCC dimension. A decomposition algorithm generates optical kernels and weights for efficient imaging. Simulations show improved accuracy and computational efficiency compared with methods assuming constant mask transmittance within source sectors.

### 26. Y. Cao et al., “Optimized hardware and software for fast full-chip simulation”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1117/12.600951  
**Source clue:** SPIE Optical Microlithography XVIII  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper describes a high-speed lithography simulation platform combining algorithmic, software, cluster, and custom hardware innovations. The platform targets full-chip RET verification and process analysis under low-k1 conditions. Using an image-processing-based approach and proprietary acceleration hardware, it supports many TCC kernels and vector or scalar optical models while achieving orders-of-magnitude speedups over conventional software-only simulation.

### 27. T. Kingsley et al., “Advances in compute hardware platforms for computational lithography”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1117/12.712397  
**Source clue:** SPIE Optical Microlithography XX  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper reviews the growth of computational lithography workloads driven by OPC, scattering bars, phase-shift masks, and lithography verification. It discusses the cost, power, cooling, and support demands of large CPU compute farms. It then considers emerging high-performance computing platforms such as multicore CPUs, FPGAs, Cell processors, DSPs, and GPUs, comparing their potential to reduce computational cost and improve throughput.

### 28. J. Cong and Y. Zou, “Lithographic aerial image simulation with FPGA-based hardware acceleration”

**DOI:** 10.1145/1344671.1344683  
**Source clue:** ACM/SIGDA FPGA 2008  
**English abstract-style summary:** This conference paper presents an FPGA-based custom accelerator for lithographic aerial image simulation using a polygon-based model. It designs memory partitioning to supply many processing elements, applies deep loop pipelining, and uses ping-pong buffers for function-block pipelining. Initial results show significant speedup over microprocessor software and highlight the need for manual architecture-level exploration even when using C-to-RTL synthesis tools.

### 29. J. Cong and Y. Zou, “FPGA-Based Hardware Acceleration of Lithographic Aerial Image Simulation”

**DOI:** 10.1145/1575774.1575776  
**Source clue:** ACM Transactions on Reconfigurable Technology and Systems  
**English abstract-style summary:** This journal article expands the FPGA acceleration work for lithographic aerial image simulation. It implements a customized FPGA accelerator using memory partitioning, deep loop pipelining, and ping-pong buffer pipelining. The design achieves notable speedup over microprocessor execution and compares FPGA acceleration with CUDA-based GPU implementation. The paper emphasizes both C-to-RTL synthesis benefits and the continuing need for architecture-level parallelism exploration.

### 30. Y. Liu, “FPGA acceleration and IC mask optimization based on deep learning”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/14727978251348626  
**Source clue:** Journal of Computational Methods in Sciences and Engineering  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper proposes a deep-learning-based mask optimization algorithm and FPGA acceleration architecture. It uses an improved GAN with multiscale feature fusion and attention to enhance mask pattern generation for proximity correction. A dynamic weighting strategy balances optimization objectives across regions. A customized FPGA design accelerates the computational bottleneck, reducing optimization time while maintaining low edge placement error.

### 31. R. Chen and V. K. Prasanna, “Energy optimizations for FPGA-based 2-D FFT architecture”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/HPEC.2014.7040967  
**Source clue:** IEEE HPEC 2014  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper optimizes FPGA-based 2-D FFT energy consumption by addressing external DRAM access inefficiencies in row-column FFT algorithms. It uses a streaming 1-D FFT kernel, balances I/O bandwidth to reduce FPGA idle time, and decomposes transposition into smaller on-chip local permutations. The optimized architecture improves energy efficiency across large 2-D FFT sizes and approaches a substantial fraction of estimated peak FPGA energy efficiency.

### 32. B. Akin et al., “Memory Bandwidth Efficient Two-Dimensional Fast Fourier Transform Algorithm and Implementation for Large Problem Sizes”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/FCCM.2012.40  
**Source clue:** IEEE FCCM 2012  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper addresses the off-chip memory bandwidth bottleneck in FPGA implementations of large 2-D FFTs. It presents algorithmic and datapath techniques for efficient DRAM access and high compute throughput under fixed bandwidth. Demonstrations include double-precision 2-D FFTs up to 2048×2048, with strong bandwidth and power efficiency compared with CPU and GPU implementations.

### 33. J. G. Nash, “High-throughput programmable systolic array FFT architecture and FPGA implementations”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCNC.2014.6785453  
**Source clue:** IEEE ICCNC 2014  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper describes a fine-grained programmable systolic FFT architecture that supports power-of-two and non-power-of-two transforms. The architecture aims for high throughput, high clock frequency, and improved signal-to-noise ratio for fixed-point word lengths. FPGA implementations demonstrate fixed-size, variable, and non-power-of-two FFT use cases, with higher throughput and clock rates than compared pipelined or memory-based FFTs.

### 34. J. G. Nash, “Distributed-Memory-Based FFT Architecture and FPGA Implementations”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics7070116  
**Source clue:** Electronics  
**English abstract-style summary:** This paper proposes a distributed-memory FFT architecture for FPGAs. The design emphasizes high clock speed, programmability, lower LUT/register usage, regular systolic structure, and support for power-of-two and non-power-of-two FFTs. Multiple designs across vendors and FPGA types show improved throughput per logic cell relative to alternatives, making the architecture attractive for scalable FPGA signal-processing systems.

### 35. J. Cong et al., “High-Level Synthesis for FPGAs: From Prototyping to Deployment”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/TCAD.2011.2110592  
**Source clue:** IEEE TCAD  
**English abstract-style summary:** The paper reviews the transition from RTL design to high-level synthesis for FPGA-based system-on-chip development. It argues that modern HLS tools have matured through broader language support, stronger compilation, platform-based modeling, and domain-specific implementation approaches. Using AutoESL/AutoPilot and Xilinx platforms as examples, it presents industrial case studies and comparisons showing productivity and resource benefits versus manual RTL design.

### 36. R. Tessier, K. Pocek, and A. DeHon, “Reconfigurable computing architectures”

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/JPROC.2014.2386883  
**Source clue:** Proceedings of the IEEE  
**English abstract-style summary:** This survey reviews reconfigurable computing architectures and their role in combining hardware performance and energy efficiency with software-like flexibility. It covers architectural concepts, compute models, tools, runtime reconfiguration, and application domains. The paper emphasizes scenarios where reconfigurable systems can meet real-time performance needs without custom ASIC fabrication and can be upgraded or specialized during their lifecycle.

### 37. M. J. Moure et al., “Advanced features and industrial applications of FPGAs—A review”

**DOI:** 10.1109/TII.2015.2431223  
**Source clue:** IEEE Transactions on Industrial Informatics  
**English abstract-style summary:** This review discusses modern FPGA features and their impact on industrial applications. It emphasizes the shift from hardware accelerators to system-on-chip platforms enabled by embedded and soft processors. It also reviews analog resources, floating-point operators, memory controllers, IP cores, and software tools. Application areas include real-time simulation, advanced control, and electronic instrumentation, especially in mechatronics, robotics, and power systems.
